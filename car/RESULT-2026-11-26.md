## What this means

Path B (moving `nbt_carplay` to the Evo) is verified up to the same point as on the old NBT: the iPhone treats the head
unit as a certified CarPlay car. Next come the parts of `nbt_carplay` that do not depend on the chip:
- `CarPlayStartSession`;
- the network over NCM (lwIP in the process);
- AirPlay: pair-setup/verify, auth-setup by the chip, H.264 screen, audio, microphone, iDrive controller.

The platform part on the Evo:
- video: mm-renderer / IVA-HD on the 1280 window;
- audio and microphone: A2I (`/opt/mm/lib/liba2i_client.so`);
- the menu entry;
- coexistence with the iPod stack: terminate the `-dipod` handlers, as done here.

## Observations for the full port

- `vdev-medialaunch` restarts the iPod handlers after every iPhone reconnect. They must be terminated again until
  configuration 6 is selected.
- The first `lockdown` after the configuration is selected needs a restart again, as on the old NBT and in the emulator.
- The RSA-2048 keys for pairing take 6–8 s to generate on the OMAP5. The pairing record in RAM is lost when the head
  unit restarts, and "Trust?" will ask again. For the full port it must be stored permanently, but that is a deliberate
  write to `/var`, to be decided later.

## Third car visit, 20:28–20:51: "as the factory does it" — the factory CarPlay showed a picture over the cable ✅ (partly)

Mode `nbt_carplay --bridge-start` (SHA-256 `76627046…`), stage `car-chip-test.sh bridge-start`. Logs: `bridge.txt`,
`bridge-v4-run1.txt`, `sloginfo-after-bridge.txt`, `cp-bridge.pcap` in the same folder.

| Step | What was found |
|---|---|
| Firewall | `pf`: `block all`, pass only by interface names (`uap0` — the CarPlay ports, IPv4 only). For the run, one rule `pass quick on tap0 all` in the empty anchor `filter_USB` (RAM), afterwards `pfctl -a filter_USB -F rules` (checked: 0 rules) |
| IPv4 | the factory `mdnsd` takes only interfaces with IPv4 (`SetupInterfaceList … Family: 2`); the iPhone asks for an address over DHCP by itself on the cable. The bridge gives tap0 `172.16.226.1/24` and answers the iPhone's DHCP with `172.16.226.100` (like `dhcpd.uap0.conf`: 3 days, no gateway) |
| Bonjour | with IPv4 the factory `mdnsd` at once announced `CarPlay._airplay._tcp` on tap0 (port 7000, TXT `deviceid features fv flags model pi srcvers=280.33.8`, **without `pk`**) and itself looks for `_carplay-ctrl` |
| Start | `CarPlayStartSession` → `[172.16.226.1]:7000`, tap0 MAC, no key, version 280.33.8. The iPhone connected to the **factory** receiver: 12.5 MB in ~40 s |
| Screen | head unit log: `Modes changed: screen controller` — **the factory CarPlay on the head unit screen** (the owner saw it). After ~15 s the owner minimised it: `Setting INIT resource mainScreen take userInitiated` → `Screen stream stopped`, the session lived on (`mainAudio controller`) |
| Minus | there is **no Apple CarPlay entry** in iDrive and you cannot return to the screen: the HMI does not know about the device — the session came around the Connectivity "device manager" (the factory path: iPhone over BT → CarPlay device → menu entry → screen request) |
| End | tap0 removed → `kTimeoutErr` → `CarPlay Session terminated` (normal). Our process crashed on exit (`SIGSEGV ip=0`, core dumped) — a bug in our shutdown. **After that the head unit restarted**; the cause has not been checked yet |

During the run **Bluetooth and Wi-Fi were off** in the iPhone — only the cable: the factory receiver accepted a session
from an iPhone that Connectivity does not know over BT.

**The restart (checked after boot, read only):** of the new files in `/var/dump` there is only `nbt_carplay.core` (98 KB,
18:50 — our crash), next to it at 18:50 `save/dumperResetHist.txt` appeared, at 18:51 `core.counter` was updated, there
is no Connectivity core dump. So the head unit almost certainly restarted **because of the core dump of our process**
(on this head unit any core dump leads to a restart). The cause of the crash was found and fixed: after tap0 was
removed, frames from the iPhone went to lwIP, which is not running in bridge mode (`netif.input` = NULL). Lesson: any
crash of our program in the car = a head unit restart.

Next: (1) ~~check after boot~~; (2) ~~crash on exit~~ (build `f55d821a…`); (3) find out what the HMI needs for the
"Apple CarPlay" entry (a device in Connectivity: iPhone over BT with wireless CarPlay,
`updateCarPlayDeviceInfo`/DipoState) — the next run with the full `sloginfo -w` log during the session.

## Fourth car visit, 21:00–21:07: cable while wireless CarPlay is active ❌

The iPhone with Bluetooth (Wi-Fi in it is "off", but wireless CarPlay connected by itself). The log of the factory
wireless connection is `sloginfo-wireless-baseline.txt` (the analysis is in `factory-like-design-2026-11-26.md`, last
section: the menu entry appears at the step `Adding/Updating Controller in view`, the session is requested by
Connectivity through the iPhone's `_carplay-ctrl`).

- Run 0: after the head unit restart the pairing from RAM was gone, and the locked iPhone answered `PasswordProtected`
  (`bridge-wl-run0.txt`). Keep the iPhone unlocked.
- Run 1 (`bridge-wl-run1-ipv6.txt`, `slog-live-wl-run1-ipv6.txt`): pairing, iAP2, `CarPlayStartSession` with the IPv6
  address of tap0 — the iPhone did not connect, it stayed on Wi-Fi.
- Run 2 (`bridge-wl-run2-ipv4.txt`, `slog-live-wl-run2-ipv4.txt`): the same with `172.16.226.1` (as in the successful
  run) — the iPhone did not connect either; the iPhone does not announce `_carplay-ctrl` on the cable.

**Conclusion:** while a wireless session is running, the iPhone ignores `CarPlayStartSession` over the cable. Binding
the wired session to a "device in the list" through simultaneous BT/Wi-Fi will not work this way. Next, without the
car: analysis of `libconn_carplaydevctrl.so`/Connectivity (how to add a device to the list without BT), and the option
of announcing `_carplay-ctrl` on tap0 on behalf of the iPhone. By default the address in `CarPlayStartSession` is now
the receiver's IPv4 (`NBT_CP_START_V6=1` — IPv6). Everything was removed from the head unit RAM (`cleanup`), 0 rules in
`filter_USB`.
